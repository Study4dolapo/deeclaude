In 1986, a lake in Cameroon killed more than seventeen hundred people in a single night.

No fire. No flood. Not even a sound.

So what happened?

Lake Nyos sits inside a volcanic crater. And beneath its calm surface, something was building.

For centuries, carbon dioxide seeped up from the magma below, dissolving into the cold bottom water. Trapped under pressure. Like a sealed bottle of soda.

Then, on August 21st, something disturbed the layers.

And the lake let go.

The gas escaped all at once. Heavier than air, it spilled over the crater rim and rolled down the valleys. Silently. Into villages. Into homes.

People went to sleep… and never woke up.

Today, pipes in the middle of the lake vent the gas, one slow fountain at a time.

Because the volcano below has never stopped feeding it.
