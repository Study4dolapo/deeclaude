In 1986, a lake in Cameroon killed more than seventeen hundred people in a single night.

There was no fire. No flood. Not even a sound.

So what happened?

Lake Nyos sits inside the crater of a volcano. And for centuries, something was building beneath its calm surface.

Carbon dioxide, seeping up from magma deep below, quietly dissolved into the lake's cold bottom water. Trapped under pressure. Like a sealed bottle of soda.

Then, on August 21st, something disturbed the layers.

And the lake let go.

The gas escaped all at once. Heavier than air, the cloud spilled over the crater rim and rolled down the valleys. Silently. Into villages. Into homes.

People went to sleep… and never woke up.

Today, pipes rise from the middle of the lake, venting the gas one slow fountain at a time.

Because the volcano below has never stopped feeding it.
