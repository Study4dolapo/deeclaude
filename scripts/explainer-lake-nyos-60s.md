**[0:00]** One night in 1986, a lake in Cameroon killed more than seventeen hundred people.

**[0:06]** No fire. No flood. No sound.

**[0:09]** Lake Nyos sits inside a volcanic crater.

**[0:12]** For centuries, carbon dioxide seeped up from magma deep below, dissolving into the cold bottom water.

**[0:19]** The lake held it under pressure. Like a sealed bottle of soda.

**[0:24]** Then, on August 21st, something disturbed the layers.

**[0:29]** The gas escaped all at once.

**[0:32]** A cloud heavier than air spilled over the crater rim and rolled down the valleys.

**[0:38]** It moved silently. Into villages. Into homes.

**[0:43]** People went to sleep and never woke up.

**[0:47]** Today, pipes rise from the middle of the lake, venting the gas one slow fountain at a time.

**[0:54]** Because the volcano below is still feeding the lake.
